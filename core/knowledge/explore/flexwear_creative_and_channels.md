# Sample Brand Deep-Dive: FlexWear - Creative System, Channels & Performance

Continuation of the FlexWear worked example (see `flexwear_brand_and_positioning.md` and `flexwear_market_and_personas.md`) - illustrative/fictional brand, representative real-world structure.

## Creative system: narratives, frameworks & hooks

Six narrative arcs, chosen by persona and product line:
1. **Comfort-First Entry** - Meera, Core line: leads with how the product feels, not what it does; the gym is a backdrop, not the pitch.
2. **Performance-Proof** - Aisha, Power line: specific, verifiable claims (compression level, sweat-wicking test result) over slogans.
3. **Fit-For-Real-Bodies** - all personas: addresses the sizing/fit gap directly - real bodies, real size range shown, not one idealized body type.
4. **Versatility / Athleisure Crossover** - Priya: one outfit shown across gym, errands, and WFH in a single piece of creative.
5. **Fabric-Tech Education** - explains what "4-way stretch" or "moisture-wicking" actually means in practice, for buyers who want substance over hype.
6. **UGC Community Proof** - real customers, real workouts, unscripted - lowest production value, often highest trust.

Hook-writing rule (same as the general framework): a tease raises one doubt resolved later ("Comfort-First," "Fit-For-Real-Bodies"); a thesis states the whole argument up front ("Fabric-Tech Education"). Know which shape is being written.

Hook bank (illustrative examples of the pattern - generate new ones in this style rather than repeating these):
- "You don't have to earn comfort" (tease, Meera)
- "600 squats later, still no slipping" (thesis/proof, Aisha)
- "Cut for one body type. We tested ours on many." (thesis, Fit-For-Real-Bodies)
- "One outfit. Gym, errands, and the 9am call." (tease, Priya)

Cross-cutting production rules: never show a "before" body · real customers in real settings outperform posed studio shots for this brand · a visible size range in group shots is a trust signal, not just representation · fabric-tech claims always paired with what it means in practice, not the spec alone.

## Channel playbook

| Channel | Buyer profile | Implication for creative/media |
|---|---|---|
| D2C website | Most engaged, size-guide-reading buyer | Best for full narrative + detailed size/fit education |
| Instagram/TikTok | Discovery-led, UGC and creator-partnership heavy | Less compliance-constrained than health categories - creative velocity can be much higher here |
| Myntra/Amazon | Assortment- and review-driven; buyers compare across brands directly | PDP needs to win on review credibility and clear size charts, not brand story |
| Fitness influencer partnerships | Aisha-persona audience, performance-credibility transfer | Works best when the creator's own training style genuinely matches the product's positioning, not a generic paid mention |

## Retention & lifecycle

The highest-leverage retention lever here isn't habit formation (as in a consumable category) - it's satisfaction with fit and durability on the first purchase. A poor first-fit experience (wrong size, fabric that doesn't hold up after a few washes) is the dominant driver of non-repeat, more than price or competitor pull.

Practical implication: a first-purchase communication that reinforces correct sizing (not just "true to size" but real guidance on how to choose between two close sizes) reduces the return/non-repeat rate more than any post-purchase discount offer would.

## Performance measurement

Same hook/hold/CTR diagnostic funnel as the general framework applies. Two differences from a health/nutrition category:
- **RTO drivers differ**: returns here are overwhelmingly fit/size-driven, not COD-trust-driven - track return reason codes separately from general RTO rate.
- **Repeat purchase is durability-gated**: a customer who had one item hold up well across several months of wash cycles is a much stronger repeat-purchase signal than early engagement metrics (hook rate, CTR) - weight lifecycle-stage read accordingly, same principle as the general framework's "diagnose in funnel order, not top-line ROAS alone."

If asked to predict performance for a specific creative/channel combination with no real precedent, say so plainly rather than inventing a number.
