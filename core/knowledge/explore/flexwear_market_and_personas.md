# Sample Brand Deep-Dive: FlexWear - Market Landscape & Customer Personas

Continuation of the FlexWear worked example (see `flexwear_brand_and_positioning.md`) - illustrative/fictional brand, representative real-world structure.

## Market landscape & competitive position

The Indian activewear market splits into two incumbent groups: global sports brands (Nike, Adidas, Puma) with strong brand recall but sizing/fit cut for a different average body shape, and India-native mass brands (Cultsport, HRX) with accessible pricing but positioned more on price than fabric quality or fit precision.

FlexWear's wedge sits between them: premium fabric quality and genuinely inclusive, India-fit-tested sizing, at a price closer to the mass tier than the global sports tier. The recurring customer complaint this addresses - imported activewear running narrow or short for the Indian body - is a well-known category gap, not a claim FlexWear needs to manufacture; it just needs to be demonstrated (real size-chart transparency, real fit photos across body types) rather than asserted.

Never name a competitor in ad, PDP, or static copy; unnamed structural contrast is fine ("most activewear is cut for one body type - we test ours on many").

## Customer intelligence: who we're talking to

Three personas, all reachable through the same core product line but requiring different entry hooks:

| Persona | Who she is | Core motivation | What earns her trust | She must never feel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Meera - Reluctant Beginner | 26-34, new or returning to fitness, intimidated by gym culture | Wants to move without feeling watched or judged | Comfort-first messaging, real (not idealized) bodies in the creative | Shamed, unfit, like she has to "earn" the right to wear the product |
| Aisha - Consistent Grinder | 24-32, trains 4-5x/week, skeptical of marketing hype | Performance and durability - does it actually hold up | Specific fabric-tech proof, real reviews, technical detail over slogans | Talked down to with vague claims she can't verify |
| Priya - Athleisure Everyday | 28-40, wears activewear as daily wear, not just for workouts | Versatility - gym to errands to WFH without changing clothes | Styling versatility shown across real contexts, not just gym shots | Boxed into "fitness person" framing when she just wants comfortable, good-looking daily wear |

How a buyer actually arrives: rarely from a cold, pure-performance need. Most first purchases are triggered by a specific friction with a current brand - sizing that didn't fit, fabric that didn't hold up after a few washes, or simply wanting something that looks good outside the gym too. Meeting that specific friction point directly (a size-chart callout, a durability demonstration) converts better than a generic "level up your workout" pitch.

One proof point that works across all three personas: a real customer wearing the product in a context that isn't a posed gym shot (running errands, working from home, a casual outing) - it does more to build "this fits into my actual life" trust than any performance claim.
