# Sample Brand Deep-Dive: GlowLabs - Creative System, Channels & Performance

Continuation of the GlowLabs worked example (see `skincare_brand_and_compliance.md` and `skincare_market_and_personas.md`) - illustrative/fictional brand, representative real-world structure.

## Creative system: narratives, frameworks & hooks

Five narrative arcs, chosen by persona and SKU:
1. **Ingredient Transparency** - Priya (Label-Reading Skeptic): "here's exactly what's in it and why," specific percentages over vague superlatives.
2. **Routine Education** - Ritu (Routine Builder): what order to use products in, and what problem each one actually solves - category education before product pitch.
3. **Sensitive-Skin Reassurance** - Ananya: full ingredient list visible, patch-test guidance, real reports from similar skin types.
4. **Review-Mining / Specific Proof** - any persona: turning a specific, textured customer quote ("didn't feel sticky, noticed less dullness after two weeks") into the hook itself, since specificity is what converts a skeptical buyer.
5. **Before/After with Reality Check** - use sparingly and always paired with a "results may vary" disclaimer and no fixed guaranteed timeline; never the sole narrative for a launch.

Hook-writing rule (same as the general framework): a tease raises a doubt resolved later ("Ingredient Transparency" - "don't buy your next face wash without reading this"); a thesis states the argument up front ("Routine Education" - "you don't need 10 steps, you need these 3, in this order").

Hook bank (illustrative examples of the pattern - generate new ones in this style rather than repeating these):
- "Don't buy your next face wash without reading this" (tease, Ingredient Transparency)
- "5% Niacinamide. Here's what that actually means for your skin." (thesis, Ingredient Transparency)
- "You don't need 10 steps. You need these 3, in this order." (thesis, Routine Education)
- "Didn't feel sticky. Noticed less dullness in two weeks." (review-mined, Specific Proof)

Cross-cutting production rules: real skin textures shown, not over-retouched · every ingredient-percentage or SPF claim on-screen must match the actual formulation · before/after content always carries a visible "results may vary" disclaimer · zero winning creative relies on skin-shaming as the hook.

## Channel playbook

| Channel | Buyer profile | Implication for creative/media |
|---|---|---|
| D2C website | Most engaged, ingredient-research-led buyer | Best for full routine education and ingredient-transparency content |
| Instagram/TikTok | Discovery-led; skinfluencer and dermat-adjacent creator content performs well | Creator credibility matters more here than production polish |
| Amazon/Nykaa | Review-reading, comparison-shopping buyer | PDP needs to win on review specificity and ingredient-list clarity, not brand story alone |
| Quick-commerce | Need-now / restock intent for existing buyers | Not a discovery channel - optimize for fast recognition, not persuasion |

## Retention & lifecycle

The highest-leverage retention lever is realistic expectation-setting on timeline. Skincare results are gradual, and a first-purchase communication that sets a believable timeframe (weeks, not days) for when to expect to notice a difference reduces early drop-off from buyers who judge too soon and assume the product "isn't working."

A second lever: routine context. A buyer who understands how a product fits into a broader routine (what to use it with, what order) is more likely to keep using it consistently than one who bought it as an isolated purchase with no usage context.

## Performance measurement

Same hook/hold/CTR diagnostic funnel as the general framework. One category-specific note: claims specificity (a real percentage, a real timeframe with a disclaimer) tends to outperform generic superlatives in this category precisely because the audience has been trained by years of overclaiming competitors to distrust vague praise - this is a directional pattern, not a guaranteed number, and should be stated with appropriate confidence rather than as a fixed benchmark.

If asked to predict performance for a specific creative/channel combination with no real precedent, say so plainly rather than inventing a number.
