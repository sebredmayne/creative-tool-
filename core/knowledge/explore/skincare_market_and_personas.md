# Sample Brand Deep-Dive: GlowLabs - Market Landscape & Customer Personas

Continuation of the GlowLabs worked example (see `skincare_brand_and_compliance.md`) - illustrative/fictional brand, representative real-world structure.

## Market landscape & competitive position

The Indian D2C skincare category is crowded and ingredient-education-led - brands compete on demonstrating formulation quality and transparency more than on brand heritage, since most successful challengers here are under a decade old. The category's incumbents split into legacy mass brands (broad distribution, low ingredient specificity) and a dense field of newer D2C "clean label" challengers competing on very similar ingredient stories (Vitamin C, Niacinamide, SPF are near-universal core actives across the category).

Because the ingredient story itself is now table stakes rather than a differentiator, the real wedge is proof and specificity: showing the actual formulation percentage, real before/after context with disclaimers, and real review-sourced language, rather than repeating the same generic "clean ingredients" claim every competitor also makes.

Never name a competitor in ad, PDP, or static copy - unnamed structural contrast only ("most face washes strip natural oils - this one doesn't" rather than naming a brand).

## Customer intelligence: who we're talking to

| Persona | Who she is | Core anxiety | What earns her trust | She must never feel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Priya - Label-Reading Skeptic | 22-32, urban, has been burned by products that overpromised | "Is this actually different, or just repackaged marketing" | Specific ingredient percentages, real reviews with texture/sensation detail | Sold to with vague superlatives she can't verify |
| Ritu - Routine Builder | 25-35, building a multi-step routine for the first time | Overwhelmed by how many products and steps exist | Clear guidance on order-of-use and what each product actually does | Made to feel she needs 10 products to "do it right" |
| Ananya - Sensitive-Skin Cautious | Any age, history of reactions/irritation | Fear of a new product causing a breakout or reaction | Full ingredient transparency, patch-test guidance, real reports from similar skin types | Pressured into trying something without being able to check what's in it first |

How a buyer actually arrives: rarely a cold purchase - most first-time buyers are already using a comparable product and are looking for a specific improvement (less oily, less dull, better sun protection) or reacting to something a current product isn't delivering. The switch trigger is usually a specific unmet need, not general skincare curiosity.

The strongest cross-persona proof point: real, specific reviews describing texture, sensation, and a believable timeframe ("didn't feel sticky, noticed less dullness after about two weeks") - vague praise ("amazing product!!") converts far less than specific, checkable detail, because the skeptical-buyer instinct in this category is trained by years of overclaiming competitors.
