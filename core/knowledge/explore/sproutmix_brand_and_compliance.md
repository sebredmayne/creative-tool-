# Sample Brand Deep-Dive: SproutMix - Brand Voice & Compliance Gate

This is a worked example of what a real, compliance-heavy D2C brand playbook looks like in depth - brand voice, the compliance rules every piece of creative must clear, and how a mature brand encodes claim discipline into its creative process. "SproutMix" and "Sunny Sprout" are illustrative/fictional; the structure and rigor are representative of real kids'-nutrition D2C brands operating under strict advertising regulation.

## Brand foundation & voice

Sunny Sprout is the parent brand; SproutMix is the product line (SM2, SM7, SM13). Brand purpose: make parenting emotionally lighter - the promise is confidence for the parent, not a guaranteed outcome for the child. Tagline: "Approved by moms, loved by kids" (brand-level only - never quantify or attach a number to it).

The brand archetype is Caregiver (primary: "I will always be here for you") layered with Sage (secondary: "the truth will help you"). Every piece of creative should read as one of those two voices, and actively avoid their shadow sides:
- Caregiver shadow to avoid: guilt-tripping, fear-based manipulation, creating dependency on the product.
- Sage shadow to avoid: preachiness, lecturing, talking down to the parent.

Tone - do: warm, direct, helpful, mum-respectful truths ("kids reject what tastes like medicine"), premium cues (transparency, calm reassurance, clean-ingredient logic). Tone - avoid: brochure/clinical voice, influencer hype, generic praise, parent-shaming, cheap urgency, discount-first storytelling.

Pricing & premium positioning: no price-led hook below ₹999. Never use urgency language - hurry, last chance, lowest ever, only today, crazy deal, loot, steal price - even in a retargeting or offer static. Preferred framing: "Try the routine," "Switch with confidence," "Value pack for daily use," "Built for the daily nutrition gap."

Design system (statics/PDP): Eggshell White (60%) + Kale Green (30%) + Spinach Green as an accent (≥10%). No gradients. CTA = Kale Green background, Eggshell White label. Headlines in Caros Bold, ≤4 words at 1080px.

The gut-check for anything produced: Does it reduce a parent's anxiety instead of adding to it? Does it build trust without overclaiming? Is the SKU and age band correct? Is every claim supported, softened, or dropped? Does it still feel premium, warm, and mom-respectful - no cheap urgency, no fear or guilt? Would this still feel right for the brand in three years?

## Product line at a glance

Three age-banded SKUs, each with its own approved claims - a claim never moves from one SKU to another (see flag F5 below).

| | SM2 - Sunny Sprout 2+ (ages 2-6) | SM7 - Sunny Sprout 7+ (ages 7-12) | SM13 - ActiveSprout (ages 13+) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Serving | 2 scoops / 30g / day, split into 2 | 2 scoops / 34g / day, split into 2 | 2 scoops / 34g / day |
| Protein per serve | 5g | 9g | 12g |
| Sweetener | Jaggery & dates | Jaggery & dates | Stevia only |
| Sweetener claim | "No refined sugar, naturally sweetened with jaggery & dates" - never "no/zero/sugar-free" | Same as SM2 - never "no added sugar" | "No added sugar" IS approved here (Stevia-based) |
| Gluten | Trace barley/oats - never bare "Gluten Free" | Same as SM2 | "Naturally Gluten Free" (keep the word "Naturally") |
| Distinct approved line | - | "2.5x more protein than regular milk mixes" (SM7 only - never inflate, never name a competitor) | Biotin "for skin and hair health"; energy claims link to protein only |

A fourth product, the Immunity Boosting Kit / gummies (SBG), is always positioned as a supplement, never as candy - never "give instead of candy" or craving-based hooks.

Mandatory on every piece of communication, verbatim, never softened or paraphrased: **"Use only under medical advice by a physician, certified dietician or nutritionist."**

The product underwent a reformulation (protein source, DHA and Vitamin D levels, and some SKU-specific ingredients changed). Always use the current values above - never reintroduce a retired claim (100% Vitamin D3, "5x more Vitamin D," 100mg DHA, oat/chia/flaxseed mentions, a retired enzyme-blend name) even if it appears in older creative; see flag F4.

## The compliance gate - non-negotiable

Every piece of creative - script, static, PDP copy, influencer brief - must clear this checklist before it ships. It exists for two reasons at once: advertising-regulation risk, and a retention problem the brand has explicitly linked to acquisition - a narrative that sets a fast, checkable outcome expectation brings in parents who churn when the product can't show that outcome on their timeline.

Run the "no fast-observable-outcome" intent gate first, before writing anything: ask what a parent would expect to observe, and by when. If the angle implies height, weight, "taller," "won't get sick this winter," "focus in a month," sleep, or any other visible result on a short timeline - reject the angle, however well it might perform. This catches approved-sounding phrasing too: "supports healthy development" still implies an observable growth outcome, and fails the same way. Protein is the approved substitute for growth intent.

Then check against the flags:

| Flag | Fails if | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Prohibited outcome language - boosts / increases / ensures / guarantees / cures / prevents / "clinically proven" / a result "in X days" - even softened ("helps boost") | Soft verbs only: supports, helps, aids, helps improve |
| F2 | Any height, weight, "taller," growth-chart or percentile framing, literal or implied | Remove; reframe to ingredient/nutrition-gap; use protein for growth intent |
| F3 | A diagnostic chain - symptom → deficiency → product ("3 signs of calcium deficiency... so switch") | "Contains calcium and Vitamin D3 to support bone health" |
| F4 | A stale claim from before the reformulation | Use current values only |
| F5 | A claim moved to the wrong SKU (9g protein on SM2, "no added sugar" on SM2/SM7, "2.5x" outside SM7) | Match every claim to its own SKU only |
| F6 | Any "no/zero/sugar-free/no added sugar" wording on SM2 or SM7 | "No refined sugar, naturally sweetened with jaggery & dates" |
| F7 | "100% natural," "all natural," or "made with natural ingredients like almonds" | "Made with almonds and walnuts" (name the specific ingredients) |
| F8 | The disclaimer missing or paraphrased | Insert verbatim: "Use only under medical advice by a physician, certified dietician or nutritionist." |
| F9 | Any competitor named, or identifiable in frame | Remove entirely; unnamed structural contrast only ("most milk mixes have refined sugar in the first 3 ingredients") |
| F11 | Copy attacks sugar itself (quantity or presence) as the villain, while the SKU being sold also contains sugar | Attack refined/artificial sugar and dilution, never sugar itself: "refined sugar and artificial flavours took over - the good stuff got diluted" |
| F12 | A claim that the parent will recognize every ingredient | Name only the specific ones meant: "made with jaggery, dates, almonds and other everyday ingredients" |
| F13 | "Real food" or any framing implying the product replaces a meal | "Made with superfoods like ragi, bajra, and dates" - describe ingredients, never claim meal-replacement |

A doctor or expert on camera raises the bar, not lowers it - an unprovable line is worse coming from an authority figure, not better. F1-F3 get checked more strictly for doctor/expert-format scripts, not less.

Absolutely prohibited phrases, all products, no exceptions: No added sugar · Zero/0 sugar · Sugar-free · 100% Natural · Clinically Tested/Proven · Boosts immunity · Increases height / makes kids taller · Complete nutrition / replaces a balanced diet · Real food · Improves focus or memory / makes kids smarter · "In X days your child will..." · "Better than [competitor]" · "India's #1" (without written substantiation).

Response protocol for anything submitted: state one of three verdicts alongside the creative - **Proceed** (all clear), **Soft warning** (sound, but flag a specific claim for the brand team to verify before it goes live), or **Hard stop** (couldn't produce a compliant version - say exactly what's blocking it and what's needed). A hard stop should be rare; if it's happening often, the brief itself likely needs a different angle, not a compliance workaround.

Customer language is never a claim. "My kid grew taller," "she's not tired anymore," "his immunity improved" are all real, authentic-sounding things parents say in research - and none of them are usable as a brand claim. Approved claims always outrank customer verbatim, however good the quote sounds.
