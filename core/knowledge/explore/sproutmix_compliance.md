# SproutMix - Compliance & Claim Rules (Non-Negotiable)

These rules are loaded deterministically into every SproutMix request's system prompt - they
do not depend on vector search ranking them highly, and they are never optional or "one input
among several." If anything else in the retrieved knowledge or the user's request conflicts
with a rule here, this file wins.

Every piece of creative - script, static, PDP copy, influencer brief - must clear this
checklist before it ships. It exists for two reasons at once: advertising-regulation risk, and
a retention problem the brand has explicitly linked to acquisition - a narrative that sets a
fast, checkable outcome expectation brings in parents who churn when the product can't show
that outcome on their timeline.

Run the "no fast-observable-outcome" intent gate first, before writing anything: ask what a
parent would expect to observe, and by when. If the angle implies height, weight, "taller,"
"won't get sick this winter," "focus in a month," sleep, or any other visible result on a short
timeline - reject the angle, however well it might perform. This catches approved-sounding
phrasing too: "supports healthy development" still implies an observable growth outcome, and
fails the same way. Protein is the approved substitute for growth intent.

This applies to what you write about the idea, not just the ad copy itself: when your own
rationale or concept name explains that you avoided an outcome claim, do not name the specific
outcome word to say so (e.g. never write "avoids growth outcomes" or title something "...
Focus" or "... Growth") - describe the avoidance neutrally instead ("avoids implying a
physical or cognitive outcome"). A rationale that repeats the exact word it claims to avoid
isn't actually compliant, it's just laundering the same claim through a different field.

Then check against the flags:

| Flag | Fails if | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Prohibited outcome language - boosts / increases / ensures / guarantees / cures / prevents / "clinically proven" / a result "in X days" - even softened ("helps boost") | Soft verbs only: supports, helps, aids, helps improve |
| F2 | Any height, weight, "taller," growth-chart or percentile framing, literal or implied | Remove; reframe to ingredient/nutrition-gap; use protein for growth intent |
| F3 | A diagnostic chain - symptom → deficiency → product ("3 signs of calcium deficiency... so switch") | "Contains calcium and Vitamin D3 to support bone health" |
| F4 | A stale claim from before the reformulation | Use current values only |
| F5 | A claim moved to the wrong SKU (9g protein on SM2, "no added sugar" on SM2/SM7, "2.5x" outside SM7) | Match every claim to its own SKU only |
| F6 | Any "no/zero/sugar-free/no added sugar" wording on SM2 or SM7 | "No refined sugar, naturally sweetened with jaggery & dates" |
| F7 | "100% natural," "all natural," or "made with natural ingredients like almonds" | "Made with almonds and walnuts" (name the specific ingredients) |
| F8 | The disclaimer missing or paraphrased | Insert verbatim: "Use only under medical advice by a physician, certified dietician or nutritionist." |
| F9 | Any competitor named, or identifiable in frame | Remove entirely; unnamed structural contrast only ("most milk mixes have refined sugar in the first 3 ingredients") |
| F11 | Copy attacks sugar itself (quantity or presence) as the villain, while the SKU being sold also contains sugar | Attack refined/artificial sugar and dilution, never sugar itself: "refined sugar and artificial flavours took over - the good stuff got diluted" |
| F12 | A claim that the parent will recognize every ingredient | Name only the specific ones meant: "made with jaggery, dates, almonds and other everyday ingredients" |
| F13 | "Real food" or any framing implying the product replaces a meal | "Made with superfoods like ragi, bajra, and dates" - describe ingredients, never claim meal-replacement |

A doctor or expert on camera raises the bar, not lowers it - an unprovable line is worse coming
from an authority figure, not better. F1-F3 get checked more strictly for doctor/expert-format
scripts, not less.

Absolutely prohibited phrases, all products, no exceptions: No added sugar · Zero/0 sugar ·
Sugar-free · 100% Natural · Clinically Tested/Proven · Boosts immunity · Increases height /
makes kids taller · Complete nutrition / replaces a balanced diet · Real food · Improves focus
or memory / makes kids smarter · "In X days your child will..." · "Better than [competitor]" ·
"India's #1" (without written substantiation).

Response protocol for anything submitted: state one of three verdicts alongside the creative -
**Proceed** (all clear), **Soft warning** (sound, but flag a specific claim for the brand team
to verify before it goes live), or **Hard stop** (couldn't produce a compliant version - say
exactly what's blocking it and what's needed). A hard stop should be rare; if it's happening
often, the brief itself likely needs a different angle, not a compliance workaround.

Customer language is never a claim. "My kid grew taller," "she's not tired anymore," "his
immunity improved" are all real, authentic-sounding things parents say in research - and none
of them are usable as a brand claim. Approved claims always outrank customer verbatim, however
good the quote sounds.
