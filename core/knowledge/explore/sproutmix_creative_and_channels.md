# Sample Brand Deep-Dive: SproutMix - Creative System, Channels & Performance

Continuation of the SproutMix worked example (see `sproutmix_brand_and_compliance.md` and `sproutmix_market_and_personas.md`) - illustrative/fictional brand, representative real-world structure.

## Creative system: narratives, frameworks & hooks

Eight narrative arcs, chosen by persona and SKU:
1. **Milk Rejection / Fussy Eater** - Kavitha, SM2 ("My kid won't drink milk in the morning.")
2. **Clean Label / Ingredient Transparency** - Priya. A challenge frame, not a claim: "Don't buy without reading the label."
3. **No Sugar / Sweetener Anxiety** - cuts across all SKUs but is strongest for SM2, where the paediatric "no sugar till 2" norm makes it a moral line, not a preference.
4. **Nutrition Gap / Ingredient Education** - high-performing but high claim-risk; must avoid the F3 diagnostic chain, always land on "Contains X to support Y."
5. **Seasonal** (back-to-school / monsoon / summer) - season is context, never the standalone hook.
6. **Community-Led / Native Ingredients** - the South India mom; needs "why a milk mix" category education before product narrative.
7. **Protein Credibility** - Nidhi, SM7. "9g protein" as a direct hook works here because this audience already believes protein matters.
8. **Unsweetened / SM13** - Stevia-specific, its own claim territory.

Six script frameworks (pick by persona + narrative + format):
1. **Problem → Ingredient → Product** - best for milk-rejection/taste doubts (SM2/SM7, creator UGC): real parent frustration → what they wanted → product enters via ingredients → one claim + visible proof → soft CTA.
2. **Ingredient-Led Education** - for label-readers: "before you buy any milk mix, read this" → live ingredient reading → unnamed structural contrast → one claim + disclaimer line.
3. **Cultural/Family Moment** - entertainment-led; product enters via a gift or recommendation (social approval does the proof work).
4. **Doctor Directive** - a doctor names what to look for (DHA/brain, calcium+D3/bone), recommends a category of solution rather than a single branded instruction, closes on pack + disclaimer. Simple beats over-produced.
5. **Seasonal/Recipe** - a fresh reason for a warm/remarketing audience; specific, followable steps, jar visible throughout.
6. **Category Villain / Reclaimed Origin** - a brand-education framework: states the whole argument in the hook ("[ingredient] was a superfood. Then [shortcut] buried it."), gives fresh, specific science, names what corrupted the category and why (never sugar quantity itself), then the solution. Use only when teaching something genuinely new.

Hook-writing rule: every hook is either a tease (raises one doubt, resolved later - frameworks 1, 3, 5) or a thesis (states the whole argument up front - framework 6, and framework 2 for pure ingredient education). Know which one is being written before writing it; a tease dressed up as a thesis loses the mechanism that makes either shape work.

Hook bank:
- Proven: "Is there even any protein in this?" · "Look for DHA" · "Don't buy without reading the label" · "5 min healthy milkshake for kids."
- Directional (testing): "She won't touch ragi in her food. But she drinks it every morning" · "9g protein per glass" (SM7) · the Framework 6 thesis-hook shape.
- Retired - never resurface: "5x More Vitamin D" · "100% of daily Vitamin D3" · "No added sugar" (SM2/SM7) · bare "Gluten Free" · "Ensures height growth" · "Boosts immunity."

Cross-cutting production rules, video and static: mascot's face is never cropped · "naturally sweetened / zero added sugar" is the strongest trust pill available (used correctly per-SKU) · every high-performing static carries a visible authority signal (lab test, pediatrician-backed, regulatory badge) · real food shots beat illustrated · pack is visible throughout · zero winning creative uses a fear or guilt hook - this is a hard no, not a style note. Statics: one static, one narrative, headline ≤7 words, subhead 7-9 words, no body paragraph, no CTA on brand-new creative, and a trust anchor is mandatory.

## Channel playbook

| Channel | Buyer profile | Implication for creative/media |
|---|---|---|
| D2C website | The most engaged, self-selected, research-led buyer; varies a lot by acquisition source | Best channel for full-narrative creative and education-first formats; match the PDP promise to whatever specific claim brought the visitor in |
| Amazon | Competitor-aware, review-reading, price-comparing; often arrives via generic-category searches | PDP needs to win on comparison and review credibility, not just brand story |
| Quick-commerce (Blinkit etc.) | Need-now / restock intent, urban and metro-concentrated; more likely an existing buyer topping up than a new discovery | Not a discovery channel - optimize for fast recognition and availability, not persuasion |
| Flipkart & Meesho | The most price-sensitive, value-conscious buyers; lighter-formulation "Lite" variants over-index here | Value-forward creative and offer clarity matter more here than brand narrative |

Funnel-stage tagging (used on every brief): TOF (cold, first exposure) needs category or brand education; Hybrid (first exposure, pushing toward purchase) can carry a product claim alongside education; BOF (warm, close to buying) can lead with the claim and proof; Remarketing (already engaged) should be read on add-to-cart and average order value, not hook rate - a low hook rate here is expected, not a red flag.

One measurement caution specific to channel: a chunk of apparent D2C "lapsers" haven't actually churned - they've shifted to buying the same product on Amazon or quick-commerce instead (for cash-on-delivery, delivery trust, or reviews). Cross-check marketplace sales before treating a D2C drop-off as lost business.

## Retention & lifecycle

The single highest-leverage retention intervention identified so far isn't a product change - it's onboarding. A first-purchase communication that covers correct dosing, a realistic outcome timeline, routine-building, and what to actually watch for addresses the largest first-month drop-off drivers at once:
- **Underdosing silently doubles the reorder cycle.** Left to their own judgment, most parents default to one scoop instead of the recommended two - the jar lasts twice as long, the reorder window doubles, and there's no complaint or dissatisfaction signal at all; it just looks like churn. State the full serving size clearly and often.
- **First-taste rejection is often a 7-10 day adjustment period, not a verdict.** It's one of the two biggest first-month lapse drivers, especially for younger children - and parents frequently give up before the adjustment window closes. Any onboarding message should set that expectation explicitly.
- **What retains a loyalist isn't an outcome - it's continued ingredient conviction plus observed taste acceptance.** Lead retention and reorder communication with what a parent can already see ("she's drinking her milk every morning"), not with the outcome they originally hoped for.

## Performance measurement: what "good" looks like

The metric that matters is buyer-quality-adjusted CAC - customer acquisition cost adjusted for prepaid rate and return-to-origin (RTO) rate. A cheap acquisition that gets returned or cancelled isn't actually cheap. ROAS is a secondary read.

Hook rate is not a quality signal - across hundreds of ads analyzed, it shows close to zero correlation with CAC or ROAS. Don't optimize creative for hook rate, and don't kill a creative on a weak hook rate alone.

When something underperforms, diagnose in this order: ROAS → hook rate → click-through → add-to-cart % → purchase % → prepaid %. This isolates where in the funnel the actual problem sits, rather than guessing from the top-line number alone.

Directional performance bands (always state confidence level alongside a prediction, and cite the closest comparable creative available):

| Creative type | What to expect | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| UGC/voiceover, natural-ingredients angle, any SKU | CAC roughly ₹1,100-1,300, ROAS 55-65% | Medium - largest sample size |
| Regional-language + specificity (a vivid observation, not a generic claim) | Lower CAC, high prepaid rate, very low RTO | High direction, but must be rebuilt compliantly if it leans on a symptom/deficiency angle |
| Doctor/expert format, simply delivered | Best capital efficiency, doesn't fatigue as fast as UGC | High - only when it isn't over-produced |
| AI/animated seasonal | Spikes fast, fatigues fast | Medium - discount for freshness, plan a refresh cadence |
| Remarketing, any format | Read on add-to-cart/AOV; low hook rate expected and not a problem | High |
| SM7 static/carousel | - | Insufficient validated data - recommend a live test rather than a confident prediction |

If asked to predict performance for a combination with no real precedent, say so plainly rather than inventing a number - an honest "insufficient data, recommend a test" is more useful than false confidence.
