# Sample Brand Deep-Dive: SproutMix - Market Landscape & Customer Personas

Continuation of the SproutMix worked example (see `sproutmix_brand_and_compliance.md`) - illustrative/fictional brand, representative real-world structure.

## Market landscape & competitive position

The Indian kids'-nutrition milk-mix category is dominated by legacy brands - Bournvita, Horlicks, Complan, and the clinically-positioned Pediasure - who win on 20+ years of distribution and brand recall, not ingredient quality. Parents often describe a clean-label challenger brand as "healthy Bournvita" - a useful bridge for now, but not where the brand wants to live long-term.

Gritzo Supermilk is a primary direct D2C threat in this category, especially in the 7-12 age band and in Northeast India. Gritzo's marketing is growth/height-outcome-led - the opposite of an ingredient-honesty positioning. Parents who bought a growth-promising brand for a height promise it didn't deliver are a specific, well-evidenced acquisition pool - but the creative approach is "here's the real nutrition story," never naming or mocking the competitor.

Never name a competitor in any ad, PDP, or static, and never put a competitor's product in frame - this is a hard compliance flag (F9), not a style preference.

The category's real constraint right now is awareness, not product or proposition: unprompted awareness among non-users of any milk mix sits around 22%, but roughly 39% of people who become aware go on to trial, and about 80% of milk-mix non-users still believe a milk mix is worth having. The door is open - reach, not persuasion, is the bottleneck. (This kind of figure is a point-in-time read from a single research wave; treat exact numbers as directional and always confirm the latest with whoever owns the research internally.)

South India is often the most natural terrain for this category - ragi and bajra are already recognized native foods there, so the ingredient story needs no translation - but the market still needs category education ("why a milk mix at all") before a product pitch, and converts on community/word-of-mouth proof rather than label-reading.

## Customer intelligence: who we're talking to

The core buyer is a mother in her mid-to-late 30s, primarily a homemaker or managing a dual role, discovering the brand via Facebook or Instagram - not the younger, urban, Instagram-native mom that's easier to cast in a shoot. Write and cast for her voice.

Four personas, all variations of the same underlying "Conflicted Caregiver" in different contexts:

| Persona | Who she is | Core anxiety | What earns her trust | She must never feel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Priya - Label-Reading Urban Mom (primary) | 30-42, Tier 1/metro, health-conscious, let down by brands that overpromise | A background hum of "I can't pinpoint what's missing" nutritionally | Natural ingredients + one trusted mom's word-of-mouth | Judged, scared, pressured, manipulated |
| Ananya - Overlooked Caregiver | 32-45, Tier 1/metro, takes care of everyone but herself | Same child-nutrition worry, now also about herself | Trust in the child-facing product transfers to a product for her | Lectured about self-care, made to feel she's failing |
| Kavitha - Fussy-Eater / Taste-First Mom | 28-38, Tier 1/2, kids 4-12 | Doesn't want snack time to be a daily fight | Taste first, ingredients second, a trusted mom's proof | Judged, pressured to overhaul the whole diet |
| Nidhi - Protein-Seeking Teen Parent | Kids 4+, sharpest from age 7, Tier 1/2 | Protein is missing from the everyday plate; the gap is execution, not awareness | Real-kitchen recipe proof (a roti or dosa she recognizes), invisible taste/texture change | Talked down to, told to overhaul her routine rather than upgrade it |

How a parent actually arrives - the switch trigger: almost never from a standing start. A parent checks their current brand's label, spots refined sugar or corn syrup, and goes looking for something cleaner. The brand doesn't need to attack anyone - label-readers arrive on their own; the job is to be findable and legible when they do.

The universal switch language - "made with natural ingredients, no refined sugar, sweetened with jaggery" - is the #1 unprompted reason parents give for switching, across every region and cohort. It's also what retains loyalists: once a parent is convinced no alternative passes their own ingredient test, that conviction - not any observed outcome - is what keeps them buying.

Trust and proof are geographic, not universal: one region converts on community adoption ("many people are using it"), metro/Tier-1 on personal label-reading, another region on family recommendation. One national creative proof structure underperforms - localize the proof mechanic, not just the language.

One proof point works everywhere: a child asking for the product unprompted ("she asks for it herself"). It's the highest-credibility, zero-comprehension-cost signal in every market and cohort - treat child-initiated-request UGC as the safest, most universal early-funnel asset.

A meaningful sub-segment chooses a brand specifically because it doesn't over-promise. Parents burned by growth/height marketing from incumbents actively seek a brand that won't make that promise. This is why growth-forward creative doesn't just risk compliance - it repels the highest-conviction buyers even where it's technically allowed.
